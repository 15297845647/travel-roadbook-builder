# Intake, research, and route decisions

## Intake record

Capture the trip as a compact contract:

| Field | Required interpretation |
|---|---|
| Arrival | Exact local date, time, terminal/station, and whether it follows an overnight journey |
| Departure | Exact local date, time, terminal/station, and recommended arrival buffer |
| Duration | Inclusive calendar days and hotel nights; calculate both independently |
| Travelers | Adults, children, older adults, health limits, drivers, luggage |
| Pace | Maximum comfortable drive, earliest start, latest arrival, one or two bases preferred |
| Priorities | Must-see, nice-to-have, and explicitly droppable places |
| Budget | Per-room hotel band, transport preference, total target or flexibility |
| Interests | Landscape, food, culture, photography, hiking, family, art, nightlife |
| Constraints | Dietary, mobility, altitude, motion sickness, visa/permit, rental eligibility |
| Deliverables | Chat, HTML, single-file HTML, PDF, spreadsheet, or combination |

Resolve “8 days 7 nights” as eight dated day entries and seven sleep entries. A post-midnight arrival may create a hotel night even when sightseeing begins later; state the convention used.

## Evidence hierarchy

For facts that can change, use:

1. official government or operator pages;
2. official ticketing accounts or booking systems;
3. current map and transport platforms;
4. established booking platforms;
5. recent traveler reports for on-the-ground nuance.

Cross-check surprising facts with a second source. Examples include “free one-way rental,” seasonal road access, no-reservation claims, free scenic shuttles, and unusually short drive times.

For every critical fact, keep:

- claim;
- source title and URL;
- checked date;
- applicable visit date;
- certainty: confirmed, estimated, or pending release;
- action the traveler must take.

Do not overquote sources. Paraphrase and link.

## Practical use of Xiaohongshu and community sources

Use recent posts to discover:

- real queue and parking patterns;
- best visit direction and photography time;
- room-view reality versus marketing names;
- road-condition experiences;
- restaurant candidates and ordering tips;
- common failure modes and worthwhile sacrifices.

Then verify operating rules, prices, schedules, road access, and booking requirements against authoritative or current commercial sources. Note when a tip is anecdotal.

## Door-to-door transport comparison

Compare transport on the same basis:

| Factor | Include |
|---|---|
| Time | Hotel checkout, station/airport trip, security, waiting, transport, pickup, final transfer |
| Cost | Fare/rental, fuel or charging, tolls, parking, one-way fee, taxi transfers |
| Effort | Luggage changes, altitude, night driving, unfamiliar roads, number of drivers |
| Flexibility | Scenic stops, rural access, weather changes, luggage storage |
| Risk | Cancellation, delays, road closure, parking shortage, fixed departure |

Recommend rail inside dense city pairs when it saves meaningful door-to-door effort. Recommend a car where it unlocks rural or mountain access. Avoid keeping a car idle through pedestrian old towns unless pickup/return logistics justify it.

## Realistic driving model

Start with current navigation distance and time, then add:

- 10–15 minutes per fuel, charging, restroom, or checkpoint stop;
- actual meal time;
- scenic stops separately;
- city congestion, parking search, shuttle queues, and rental paperwork;
- slower progress for mountain roads, darkness, rain, snow, fog, or altitude;
- at least one recovery buffer on long days.

Label the result “realistic elapsed time,” not “pure wheel time.” If the user compares a 440 km route with a 680 km route, explicitly show which waypoints, detours, or wrong endpoints create the difference.

## Route scoring

Score candidate routes from 1–5:

- must-see coverage;
- maximum daily fatigue;
- total backtracking;
- seasonal reliability;
- hotel and dining quality;
- departure-day safety;
- cost;
- flexibility.

Give the recommended route and explain the largest compromise. Offer a relaxed version and a disruption fallback. Preserve the user's stated must-see places unless removal is required for safety or schedule feasibility.

## Rental-car specification

Record:

- platform and supplier;
- pickup/return city and exact branch or station area;
- branch opening hours;
- date and target time;
- one-way fee and whether it is promotional, quoted, or pending;
- deposit, payment card, license/translation requirements;
- mileage cap, fuel/charge policy, roadside support;
- vehicle class, luggage capacity, tires, chains, spare tire;
- base insurance, deductible, glass/tire/undercarriage coverage;
- second-driver rule;
- latest cancellation time.

Treat “异地还车免费” as unconfirmed until the chosen supplier, branch pair, vehicle group, and dates show zero fee in the final quote.

## Reservation calendar logic

For each attraction or transport product, record:

- visit date and desired entry slot;
- official channel and account name;
- release window and exact release time if published;
- traveler identity data required;
- ticket plus shuttle/boat/cable-car components;
- cancellation and modification deadline;
- backup slot, backup entrance, or backup activity;
- a reminder date to recheck if sales are not yet open.

Do not calculate release dates blindly when policies say “up to N days” or exclude the current day. Show the formula used and label uncertainty.
